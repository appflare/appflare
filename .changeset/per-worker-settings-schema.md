A catalog secret may have a `key` of its own, so an entry of several Workers can give each Worker its own value under one name (each gatekeeper's `CLIENT_ID`, say): the key is what the forms, the install's records, `derive.from`, seed params and a Pipelines sink's `tokenSecret` go by, and it defaults to the name (`secretKey`, `hasOwnSecretKey`). Secrets of one name must each list `workers`, and no Worker may get two of them; keys are unique (`secretKeyProblems`). A config patch may now set a var to text, with the placeholders a var's default takes, besides removing one; may add a Workers AI binding (`ai: { binding }`, only to a config without one); and may give a service binding to a Worker of the entry `props` (`configPatchManifestProblems`). Self and entry service bindings in an artifact may carry `props` (`serviceBindingPropsSchema`). A patched var may not share its name with a secret or a catalog var that goes to the same Worker. `requires` takes three values that name a manager feature rather than an account capability, `secret-keys`, `service-props` and `config-patch-values` (`MANAGER_FEATURE_REQUIREMENTS`, `accountRequirements`): the catalog checks require them where a key, props, patched var text or a patched `ai` binding is used, and a manager from before them leaves such an entry out of its catalog. A revision may add `secret-keys` with keys on the secrets it adds, never change a released secret's key (`secretKeysRevisionProblem`).
