Serve apps that need every name under one hostname. For an app whose manifest sets `install.wildcardHostname`, the install form's Address and the app page offer a wildcard domain: choose one of the account's domains and a hostname such as `tunnels.example.com`, and Appflare adds proxied DNS records for `tunnels.example.com` and `*.tunnels.example.com` and Workers routes for both to the app's Worker (custom domains match one exact name, so they cannot do this). The records and routes are recorded as the app's resources, kept by updates and settings changes, and removed by Remove and by uninstall before the Worker. The app page shows the domain as `*.tunnels.example.com`; health checks and Check now probe `tunnels.example.com`, and once the app answers there workers.dev is turned off as with other custom domains. Names with DNS records or routes of their own, and another Worker's custom domain, are refused rather than replaced. The dialog explains that names two levels below the domain need Total TLS or an uploaded certificate, and asks before the domain itself is used, since every name in it then reaches the app. External domains are refused for these apps: Cloudflare offers wildcard custom hostnames on the Enterprise plan only. Settings may use `{{wildcardHostname}}`: the install fills it in from the first upload, and adding or removing the wildcard domain on the app page starts a settings change that deploys the stored settings again with the new value (or says why it could not start).
